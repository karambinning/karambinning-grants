Anna, Kim and Les — the family behind Grants — have been quietly looking after King's Lynn's appetite from a side street off the Tuesday Market Place for many years now.

Who we are

The room is small. The cooking is generous.

Grants is a restaurant of the old, dependable kind: a short menu, cooked properly, served by people who know your name by your second visit. We are family-run — Anna, Kim and Les between the front and the back — and the place is shaped by the three of us, every night we open.

If you are looking for something performative, this is not it. If you want a quiet table, a glass of something, and a plate of food that lands square on what you fancied, you are very welcome.

The dining room is intimate, which is the polite word for "book ahead." Saturdays and Sundays fill quickly; midweek evenings have a softer rhythm.

How we cook

Plain English, done well.

No deconstructions. No foam. A starter is a starter. A pie is a pie. The pudding course is the point of the meal.

The cooking is traditional British, by which we mean: prawn cocktail, beef and ale pie, roast lamb on Sunday with all of it, banoffee pie when the season's right, and a few specials that move with what the suppliers are sending.

Portions are sized for hunger, not photography. The kitchen turns out food at the pace of a small team that knows what they are doing — so the evenings have a settled, unhurried feel.

Where we are

Norfolk Street, just off the market.

A short walk from the Tuesday Market Place, tucked into a row of independent shopfronts. There is a small frontage and a quiet door — once inside, the dining room opens up.

A note on bookings

The dining room seats only a handful of tables. We strongly recommend booking — particularly for Sunday lunch and any party of four or more.
